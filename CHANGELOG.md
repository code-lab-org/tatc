# TAT-C Change Log

## Unreleased

Added:
 - Optional field `communications` of space systems (`schemas.SpacecraftCommunications`: radio `Transmitter`s down to ground stations or across to relays, and how data is generated, `DataGeneration`), ground stations (`StationCommunications`: antennas and `Receiver`s), and relays (`RelayCommunications`: antennas and receivers of each relay satellite, and its feeder link to the ground), which constellations give their members. Transmitters and receivers take coarse parameters (EIRP or power and antenna, G/T or antenna and noise temperature, link modes or a Shannon modem) for link budgets, or only a rate when nothing more is known. Class `schemas.DataFlow` holds the options of data flow analyses.
 - Coarse radio link budgets (`utils.compute_link_rate`): free-space path loss, clear-sky atmospheric loss scaled from zenith by elevation, and carrier to noise density, with the rate of the fastest link mode that closes with a margin (`compute_mode_rate`) or of a modem that follows the channel's capacity less a gap (`compute_shannon_rate`). Sourced nominal frequencies and zenith attenuations of the S, X, Ku, and Ka bands (`utils.get_band`), and the modes of DVB-S2 and common CCSDS codes at a symbol rate (`utils.get_link_modes`).
 - Function `analysis.compute_data_flow`: the latencies of satellites' observations through the flow of their data, rather than timing alone. Each satellite generates data (`analysis.compute_generation`, at its communications' rate or its instruments' total data rate, while active), stores it (losing data while its storage is full), and sends it first in, first out over links to ground stations and relays at rates from link budgets (or a known rate, such as its resources' `downlink_rate`), scheduled cell by cell of a grid of time: each transmitter sends over one link at a time, ground stations and relay satellites receive over a limited number of antennas, and relay satellites forward at most their feeder rate. Links are released once storage empties (or at the end of their contacts). Data generated during a link is sent as it is generated, or, with `DataFlow(streaming=False)`, recorded and played back at the end of the contact, as on a scheduled pass. Its `DataFlowResult` gives the schedule of links, the storage of each satellite over time, a summary of volumes generated, delivered, and lost, and the utilization of each ground station and relay satellite. Functions `analysis.sample_link_geometry` and `compute_link_rates` give the distance, elevation, and rate of links through contacts on the grid of time.
 - Argument `data_flow` of `analysis.evaluate_architecture` (and `Architecture.evaluate`): the downlinks of observations through the flow of data (`lost`, and the evaluation's `flow`), with satellites generating data only while active and sharing the antennas of ground stations and relays, so that leaving out or adding members (such as a relay) measures their value to latency and data loss. Contacts and observations are not analyzed again for members that differ only in their communications or resources.
 - Argument `data_flow` of `tradespace.Observations`: studies of architectures with a data flow model, whose cached observations and contacts are shared by designs that differ only in communications or resources. The metrics of the phases of a mission with a data flow model are those within the data flow of all its planned satellites, which share antennas.
 - Catalog spacecraft, relay systems, and ground networks give communications with the source of each value, which are not part of the digests of configurations: `Spacecraft.communications` (transmitters, which `SatelliteSystem.build` gives satellites), `SatelliteSystem.services` of relaying systems (antennas and receivers of each relay satellite, which `build_relay(service=...)` gives the relay), and `GroundNetwork.terminals` (antennas and receivers of each station, which `GroundNetwork.build` gives the stations).
 - Sourced communications in the catalog: the transmitters of the stored mission data of the spacecraft of JPSS (NOAA-20 and NOAA-21), Landsat 8, Sentinel-1, -2, -3, -5P, and -6, Metop, Metop-SG A1, NISAR, SWOT, PACE, ICESat-2, SMAP, OCO-2, GCOM-W, EarthCARE, COSMIC-2, GOES-19, Himawari-9, and MTG-I1, and the crosslinks to TDRS of GPM, Terra, and TRMM; the single and multiple access services of TDRS (`build_relay(service="single_access")`); the 13 m antennas of NOAA's Fairbanks and Wallops stations; and a new catalog system `near_earth_network`: the antennas of NASA's Near Earth Network, NASA's own and those of KSAT, SSC, and SANSA (configurations `all`, `ksat`, `ssc`, and `nasa`), each with its position, G/T, and maximum rates. The systems page lists them.
 - Example notebook `ComputeDataFlow`: the latency of a constellation's observations through the flow of its data, with link budgets of an X-band downlink to KSAT's antennas of the Near Earth Network, and the value of adding a TDRS relay with a Ka-band crosslink, with figures of storage, links, and latency distributions and an animation of the data flow.
 - Validation notebook `ValidateLatencyNOAA20` tests the data flow of NOAA-20 (its cataloged instrument data rates and Ka-band playback) against the order and times its granules are created (data observed during contacts played back at their ends, without streaming), and its granule times are compared in the unit of its contacts (it failed with pandas 3).
 - `analysis.sample_field` and `compare_sampled_means` sample fields on curvilinear grids (two-dimensional latitude and longitude coordinates, as of WRF) and unstructured grids (coordinates of a dimension of cells, as of MPAS, in degrees or radians) at their nearest cells, found on the sphere (requires scipy). `sample_field` moved to the new module `analysis.field_sampling` (still exported by `tatc.analysis`).
 - Function `utils.compute_ground_sample_azimuth` and methods `PointedInstrument.compute_sample_azimuth` and `ConicalInstrument.compute_sample_azimuth`: the azimuth on the ground of a sample's along-track axis at a target, which orients its footprint. `analysis.compute_sample_sizes` gives it (`sample_azimuth`).
 - Function `analysis.compute_observation_quality`: the sample sizes of observations (see `compute_sample_sizes`), the resolution they bound along and across track (the larger of a sample and the spacing of samples, or the nominal ground sample distance where samples are not modeled), and the observing instrument's performance scaled to the slant range of each observation (`ground_sample_distance`, `noise_scale`, and `signal_to_noise_ratio`). `compute_sample_sizes` gives observations without an `instrument` column (such as limb soundings) a slant range only, rather than failing.
 - `analysis.sample_field` samples fields over the footprints of observations' samples (`method="footprint"`, a Gaussian or uniform `footprint` of the sizes and azimuth of `compute_sample_sizes`) and inside their regions (`method="area"`, as the viewed parts of features), by the mean of the field's cells weighted by the footprint and the cells' areas, at the nearest time step or interpolated between steps (`time_method`), on any grid, with the share of each footprint's weight on cells with values (`{name}_valid_fraction`, below `min_valid_fraction` no value), as at the edges of a regional field or over cells without values.
 - `analysis.sample_field` samples fields with a vertical dimension at a `level` (interpolated between levels) or as the sum of their levels weighted by `weights` that the user gives (such as layer thicknesses, weighting functions, or averaging kernels, of every observation or of each), and function `analysis.sample_profiles` samples each level of each observation in long format, or the points of observations at heights (such as the tangent points of limb scans) at their heights, interpolated between levels of given heights (such as geopotential heights).
 - Function `analysis.simulate_observation_errors`: the observed values of sampled observations with random errors of a standard deviation that the user gives, scaled by each observation's noise scale, and an optional bias, with the standard deviation of each error (`{name}_error_std`) and the observed value (`{name}_observed`), reproducible by a seed.
 - Function `preprocess.find_features` (requires `tatc[osse]`): the features of a nature run's mask at each time (contiguous cells, also across the antimeridian of a global grid), on regular and curvilinear grids, as polygons with the frame of their time (`start` and `end`) that `analysis.collect_feature_observations` takes, with their number of cells, area, equivalent diameter, area-weighted centroid, reductions of variables over their cells (minimum, maximum, mean, and integral), and optionally the label of each cell.
 - Package `tatc.export` (requires `tatc[osse]`): `to_cf_netcdf` writes observations as CF-netCDF point files (time, latitude, longitude, height above the ellipsoid, and numeric columns), and `to_ioda` writes them as the IODA files of JEDI, in the layout of JCSDA's ioda-converters (groups `MetaData`, `ObsValue`, `ObsError`, and `PreQC`, its fill values and `dateTime` units, the sensor and solar angles of the viewing geometry, and the sequence numbers of profiles), with the types and attributes of ioda's own files (int64 `Location`, variable-length string attributes, `_ioda_layout`, and units of metadata columns), read by JEDI's ioda and passing its validator.
 - Example notebook `SimulateObservations`: a day of the samples of ATMS and CrIS on NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 over the American Southwest and their quality, the GEOS-5 Nature Run averaged over each footprint (and its difference from the footprint's center), simulated errors, profiles at the tangent points of a limb sounder, and IODA files of the control and denial sets of a data denial experiment. The monsoon, hurricane, and cold pool OSSE notebooks find their features (`preprocess.find_features`), resolution elements (`compute_observation_quality`), and footprint means (`sample_field`) with the library, with the same results.
 - Function `utils.compute_atmospheric_density`: the global-mean density of the thermosphere (NRLMSIS 2.1, 100 to 1500 km) at low, moderate, or high solar activity (the long-term reference levels of ECSS-E-ST-10-04C), an index between them, or a `SolarCycle` over time (`utils.SolarActivity`), from a bundled table that `validation/atmosphere/build_density_table.py` builds with pymsis.

Changed:
 - `analysis.sample_field` (nearest) gives the values of times up to half a time step before a field's first time or after its last, as its extent reaches half a spacing beyond its outer latitudes and longitudes, rather than none.

## 3.8.0

Added architectures of observing platforms of every kind, with the ground stations and relays (such as TDRS) that receive their data and the windows in which members are active, evaluated together (`evaluate_architecture`) with the value of each member left out or candidate added, and as the designs, or a shared baseline, of trade studies (`ObservationStudy`). Added the nominal performance (ground sample distance and signal-to-noise ratio) and resources (mass, power, and data rate) of instruments and spacecraft, with sourced values in the catalog, and catalog systems of ground networks (NOAA's ground stations and NASA's Space Network). Added optional sample size grades to the validation standard (versions 8 to 11) for pushbroom, beam, geostationary, SAR, and scatterometer instruments, with sample size configurations of catalog entries, the resolutions of radars (`range_resolution` and `azimuth_resolution`), and the fixed grids of geostationary imagers (`ViewGeometry.SCAN_ALONG_FIRST`); catalog entries and systems can be listed by their tier for a use (`grade`). Fixed the longitude of `GeosynchronousOrbit` (off by up to about 0.04 deg), sample sizes of views away from their scan line, and latencies that missed a contact in progress. The default analysis of `tradespace.Observations` changed, so caches on disk of earlier studies are computed again, and every configuration of ABI, AHI, and FCI uses the inertial frame. Deprecated the field `stations` of `Architecture` (renamed `ground_stations`) and the lifetime distributions `Exponential`, `Weibull`, `Bathtub`, and `Piecewise` (renamed with the suffix `Lifetime`).

Added:
 - `schemas.Architecture` holds observing platforms of every kind (`satellites`, `aircraft`, `surface_platforms`, `radar_stations`), the `ground_stations` and `relays` that receive the satellites' data, and the `windows` (`schemas.ActiveWindow`) in which members are active, by name. Constellations given as satellites are expanded into their members. Methods `with_members`, `without_members`, `merge`, `get_member`, `get_window`, `get_active_period`, and `is_active`.
 - Class `schemas.Relay`: relay satellites, contacted by line of sight above a grazing altitude (and within a range), optionally only while the relay satellite is in view of one of its ground stations. Function `analysis.collect_relay_contacts` collects contacts with relays, which act as downlinks in `compute_latencies` (with a `relay` column).
 - Function `analysis.evaluate_architecture` (and `Architecture.evaluate`): the observations of targets by each platform within its window, the contacts of satellites with ground stations and relays while both are active, the downlink and latency of each observation, and metrics. Its `ArchitectureEvaluation` measures the value of each member, or of groups of members, by leaving it out (`leave_one_out`) and of candidates by adding each (`add_one`), without analyzing the other members again.
 - Function `analysis.collect_platform_observations`: the observations of points and regions by one platform of any kind (orbit analyses for satellites, footprints for others).
 - Catalog system `tdrs`: NASA's Tracking and Data Relay Satellites at their geostationary longitudes, with the new role `relaying` of satellite systems and `SatelliteSystem.build_relay`, which builds a `Relay` of the satellites in service. Spacecraft have a new status, `storage` (spares in orbit), which, as `retired`, is not built by default.
 - Catalog system `noaa_ground_stations` of the new class `ground_network` (`catalog.GroundNetwork`): the ground stations that receive the data of NOAA's satellites (its Command and Data Acquisition Stations at Fairbanks and Wallops, and SvalSat, McMurdo, and TrollSat), at published antenna positions, with configurations of the stations of JPSS, POES, and GOES; `build` gives `GroundStation`s, with an optional `min_elevation_angle`.
 - Catalog system `nasa_space_network`: the White Sands Complex, the ground terminals of NASA's Space Network, to give the TDRS relay its ground terminals (`build_relay(ground_stations=...)`), through which it relays from the TDRS satellites in view of White Sands.
 - Example notebook `EvaluateArchitecture`: a baseline of satellites, radars, surface stations, and ground stations from the catalog, the value of each member, and of a relay (TDRS) and a proposed satellite as candidates.
 - `tradespace.ObservationStudy` builds architectures (or satellites, as before), with an optional `baseline` architecture that every design shares; a mission plan deploys the design's satellites, and the other platforms operate in every phase within their windows. Methods `architecture_of` and `evaluation_of` (the value of each member of a design's architecture from the cached analyses). `Observations` caches the observations of platforms of any kind, the observations of members within windows, and the contacts of each satellite with each ground station and relay, and gives evaluations (`evaluate`); its argument `during_contact` sets the downlink of observations that end during a contact.
 - Optional fields `performance` (`schemas.InstrumentPerformance`: a ground sample distance and signal-to-noise ratio at a reference altitude, scaled to other altitudes by declared power laws, with `get_noise_scale` for observation errors) and `resources` (`schemas.InstrumentResources`: mass, power, and data rate, with `get_data_volume` and `total`) of instruments, which do not affect observations.
 - Optional field `resources` of space systems (`schemas.SpacecraftResources`: launch and dry mass, power, design life, storage, and downlink rate), which constellations give their members, and `SpaceSystem.get_payload_resources`, the totals of its instruments.
 - Catalog instruments and spacecraft give nominal performance and resources with the source of each value (`InstrumentEntry.performance` and `resources`, which a configuration may replace, `get_performance`, `get_resources`, and `Spacecraft.resources`), listed on the catalog's pages; they are not part of the digests of configurations. `InstrumentEntry.build` gives every instrument the performance and the instrument analyzed by default the resources, so that a satellite's totals count each instrument once, and `SatelliteSystem.build` gives satellites their spacecraft's resources.
 - Catalog sample size configurations of MWS (`beams`, one instrument for each group of channels) and TROPICS (`beams_tropics_0n`, each satellite's 118 GHz band), whose published footprints at nadir and the scan's edge are gated, and of CERES (`beams_noaa_20`, `beams_suomi_npp`), ECOSTRESS and OCI (`beams`, OCI's with the tilt schedule of its swath), and IASI-NG (`fields_of_regard`), whose only published footprints are at nadir, so that they are compared without the gate and the configurations stay partial. Validation scripts can compare published footprints without recording the gate's metric (`scanner.Setup.gate_published`) and project the pixels of a line within a scan of several lines (`scanner.Reference.line`).
 - Version 8 of the validation standard adds the sample size grade to the pushbroom class, with the gates of cross-track scanners. The grade is optional: pushbroom entries do not need it to be validated, and their sample size configurations have their own tiers, which do not set the entry's (`standard.get_required_grades`; `standard.is_compatible` takes a grade, as results of a grade added to a class apply from the version that added it).
 - Catalog sample size configurations (`pixels`) of the pushbroom imagers BBR (gated by the published footprints of its nadir and slanted views), EMIT and TROPOMI (whose only published pixels are at nadir), and OLI and MSI (nominal, from published values, as no reference data locate their detectors). Published footprints of the validation scripts can give the angle along track of a view pointed fore or aft.
 - Version 9 of the validation standard adds an optional sample size grade to the lidar, profiler, and altimeter classes, gated by published footprints and by the spacing of samples along track against that measured (`spacing_along_error`, the error of the median ratio, at most 3%).
 - Catalog sample size configurations (`footprints`) of GEDI, ATLID, and CPR, from their published divergences and beam widths, whose spacing along track is gated and published footprints compared without the gate, and of ATLAS (nominal, as its reference data hold segments rather than shots). Validation scripts of beam instruments score them with `validation_io.validate_footprints`.
 - `ViewGeometry.SCAN_ALONG_FIRST`: a `PointedInstrument` view in angles with the along-track angle first (its cross-track angular extent constant along track), as the fixed grid of a geostationary imager that sweeps about the y axis (CGMS's convention, as AHI's and FCI's); `ViewGeometry.SCAN` in the inertial frame is that of a grid that sweeps about the x axis (as ABI's). `ViewGeometry.is_scan` tells either from `FRAME`, and `compute_view_angles` takes `along_first`. `compute_sample_sizes` gives the sizes of a geostationary platform's pixels east-west (along track) and north-south (across track) in the inertial frame.
 - Version 10 of the validation standard adds an optional sample size grade to the geostationary class, with the gates of cross-track scanners. Catalog sample size configurations (`pixels`) of ABI, AHI, and FCI: the fixed grids of their infrared bands, whose published sizes at the sub-satellite point are compared without the gate, as the grids' angles define them, with pixels and spacing compared with the products' grids (`validation_io.validate_fixed_grid`). Every configuration of ABI, AHI, and FCI uses the inertial frame, in which a geostationary satellite's along-track direction is east (the coverage configurations, cones about nadir, are unchanged by it), as the configurations of an entry on a platform share its velocity frame.
 - `PointedInstrument` fields `range_resolution` (meters, in slant range, divided by the sine of the incidence angle at a target, or on the ground, `range_resolution_frame`) and `azimuth_resolution` (meters), which replace the sizes of a pixel's angles in `compute_sample_size` (and `compute_sample_sizes`), as for the resolution cells of a radar; their spacing is not modeled (`NaN`). Function `utils.compute_incidence_angle`: the incidence angle at a target on the ellipsoid.
 - Version 11 of the validation standard adds an optional sample size grade to the SAR and scatterometer classes, gated by published resolutions. Catalog sample size configurations of Sentinel-1's IW mode (`resolution_iw`, its three sub-swaths' resolutions derived from the look bandwidths and windows of its ground range detected product, compared with the Product Definition's), and nominal ones of NISAR's single-look complex products of each bandwidth, and of the 2 km product of KaRIn and the 25 km product of ASCAT (`resolution`).
 - `catalog.list_entries` takes a `grade`: the entries with a configuration for that use, whose `tier` is then the entry's for the grade (`InstrumentEntry.get_tier`, the lowest tier of its configurations that serve the grade), so that entries partial only at another grade, such as the sample size grade, can be listed as validated for coverage (`list_entries(tier="validated", grade="coverage")`).
 - `catalog.list_systems` takes a `grade` as well: the systems with a configuration for that use, whose `tier` is then the system's for the grade (`SystemEntry.get_tier`), so that, for example, systems partial for the planes of their orbits can be listed as validated for their ground tracks (`list_systems(tier="validated", grade="ground_track")`).
 - `tradespace.Criteria` take statistics themselves as objectives, tolerances, and rules of `Across`, and `Requirement` a statistic in place of its name (such as `Requirement(Quantile("cost", 0.7), "<=", 150)`), each named by its conventional name (`cost p70`), so that criteria need not repeat the names of statistics given in `statistics`. Type aliases `criteria.Objectives` (the objectives that functions judging designs take in place of criteria), `criteria.Direction`, `criteria.Rule`, `statistics.Operator`, `simulation.Sampling`, `attribution.Share`, and `utility.Form`, and `Literal` types of the other arguments that take one of a few strings (such as `errors` of `Study` and `method` of `DesignSpace.sample`), by which editors check and complete them.

Changed:
 - The field `stations` of `schemas.Architecture` is renamed `ground_stations` (`stations` is deprecated), and its `name` is optional.
 - The default analysis of `tradespace.Observations` is `analysis.collect_platform_observations` (the same observations of satellites as `collect_observations`), so caches on disk of earlier studies are computed again.
 - `tradespace.SatelliteCache` caches analyses of platforms of any kind, relabeling the column of each platform's kind.
 - The observations that `tradespace.Observations` gives are sorted by start time, rather than by satellite.
 - `tradespace.ObservationStudy` checks, when it is defined, that the references of its parts that are names or paths (the seed of `Targets`, the start of `Observations`, the parts, growth, and masses of a `MissionPlan`, the seed of `Failures`, and the `layout`) name values of its designs, and raises a `ValueError` naming each one that does not, with the name it may be a misspelling of. Errors of misspelled names of the values of designs (`design["name"]` and `design.name`), of tolerances, and of the statistics and metrics of summaries suggest the name meant, and summaries tell names that are neither statistics nor metrics from statistics of metrics that the results lack.
 - The distributions of lives `tradespace.Exponential`, `Weibull`, `Bathtub`, and `Piecewise` are renamed `ExponentialLifetime`, `WeibullLifetime`, `BathtubLifetime`, and `PiecewiseLifetime`, apart from the utilities `ExponentialUtility` and `PiecewiseUtility`; the former names are deprecated.
 - Stratified histories of failures (`simulate_many` of a deployment, with `sampling="stratified"`) are simulated several times faster for many histories: the histories of a replicate share the permutations of the strata of each draw, rather than each computing them again (the histories are the same). The annual costs of a `tradespace.CostEstimate` (`annual`, and the peak of its `metrics`) are summed without a pivot table, about 30 times faster.
 - A function of a study's results (such as the `evaluate` of a `tradespace.Study`, or the metrics of an `ObservationStudy`) whose first parameter is named `design` is given what it is of (such as what a design builds) in it, rather than raising a `TypeError`; a later parameter named `design` is given the realized design, as before.

Fixed:
 - Sample sizes of `PointedInstrument` views in scan angles away from their scan line: a step of the cross-track angle moves the line of sight by the cosine of the target's along-track angle, along the axes of the view rotated to the target. Pixels off the scan line, such as those of a geostationary imager's fixed grid, were up to about 0.4% too wide across track within 5 deg of it (unchanged on the scan line).
 - `GeosynchronousOrbit` placed its satellite off its `longitude` by up to about 0.04 deg, by the date: its node used the apparent rather than the mean sidereal time of SGP4's TEME frame, and SGP4's deep-space (lunar and solar) terms displace the satellite at its epoch from its mean elements. The node is now solved so that the sub-satellite point at epoch is at the longitude (rotated by the argument of latitude), within 1e-6 deg; within each day of the repeat cycle, the longitude still drifts by up to about 0.006 deg. GOES's slot record is updated (its longitude error had been 0.037 deg).
 - Catalog values of sourced models (such as a spacecraft's resources) set to `None`, as a model dumped with its defaults gives them, needed a provenance, so a system's record could not be written.
 - `analysis.collect_footprint_observations` failed for the footprints of radar stations with pandas 3.
 - `analysis.compute_latencies` missed a contact in progress when another contact (with another ground station or a relay) that had not started ended first, downlinking the observation later.
 - `analysis.estimate_sampling_error` failed (with an `IndexError`) for observations without any rows, rather than giving every target no samples and no error.

## 3.7.0

Added a catalog of instruments from past and present missions, and of systems (satellite programs, navigation systems, radar networks, and surface networks) from which to build baseline systems, with standards for validating them. Added a module `tradespace` (prototype) for trade studies of constellations under uncertainty: design spaces, studies of the observations, deployment, costs, and failures of each design, criteria and Pareto efficiency, and searches with optimizers. Added analyses of the statistical sufficiency of observations (sampling errors, revisits, and local time coverage), also measured against a nature run. Added airborne observations, and analyses that combine satellites, aircraft, fixed surface platforms, and radar stations (footprints, coincidences, and observations of points and features). Fixed instruments losing their fields when serialized. Instruments now reject unknown fields, which can break code that passes misspelled or unused arguments. Columns and arguments renamed for consistency across platforms can break code that uses the former names: the viewing geometry columns `sat_alt`, `sat_az`, `sat_range`, and `sat_height` (now `platform_*`), the `station` columns of radar tracks and of downlinks and latencies (now `radar_station` and `ground_station`), the arguments `satellites` of `collect_feature_observations` and `compute_sample_sizes` and `satellite` of `compute_ground_track`; the `elevation` columns of surface and radar tracks are removed; radar station elevations are now of the ground below the antenna (whose height above it is the new `height`), above the WGS 84 ellipsoid rather than mean sea level. Deprecated `collect_platform_track` and `compute_platform_track` (renamed `collect_surface_track` and `compute_surface_track`), and `collect_multi_observations` and `collect_multi_region_observations` (use `collect_observations` and `collect_region_observations` with `instrument_index=None`).

Added:
 - Module `catalog` of instruments from past and present missions (`get_entry`, `list_entries`, with the schemas of entries in `catalog.schemas`, of their records in `catalog.records`, and the standard and its evaluation of records (`evaluate_entry`) in `catalog.standard`), starting with GOES-R ABI, Meteosat Third Generation FCI, GPM GMI, GCOM-W AMSR2, ATMS, CrIS, VIIRS, MODIS, Landsat OLI, Sentinel-2 MSI, Sentinel-1 C-SAR, NISAR L-SAR, SWOT KaRIn, the SMAP radiometer, IASI, Metop-SG IASI-NG and MWS, Sentinel-5P TROPOMI, GPM DPR, MHS, ICESat-2 ATLAS, AIRS, AMSU-A, AVHRR, SSMIS, Sentinel-3 OLCI, PACE OCI, HARP2, and SPEXone, the OMPS Nadir Mapper, Himawari AHI, CERES, ECOSTRESS, OCO-2, the Sentinel-6 Poseidon-4 altimeter, Metop ASCAT, TEMPO, GEDI, EMIT, EarthCARE ATLID, BBR, CPR, and MSI, TRMM PR, TMI, and VIRS, the microwave sounders of the TROPICS constellation of CubeSats, the limb sounders Aura MLS and TIMED SABER, and the radio occultation receivers of COSMIC-2 (TGRS) and Metop (GRAS). Configurations of limb sounders and radio occultation receivers hold a sounding (`catalog.schemas.LimbScan` or `Occultation`) instead of instruments, given as the payload's `sounding`, whose `collect_observations` calls `collect_limb_observations` (in a cycle of upward and downward scans repeating from the start of the analysis) or `collect_ro_observations` with the entry's values. `InstrumentEntry.to_satellite` and `build` give an entry's instruments with the attitude they were validated with, and `build` also the `instrument_index` to analyze them with (`None` for the parts of one view), with `to_satellite` of its payload (`InstrumentEntry.to_satellite` builds only configurations analyzed with the default index of 0); the platform modeled (`platform`) selects the configuration tested on it, and a grade (`grade`) one for its use (`select_configuration`). `catalog.build_payload` gives the instruments of several configurations (such as `"atms:swath_noaa_20"`) carried together (or the sounding of one), selected by grade and platform, which must share an attitude of the body or take one that replaces it.  - Analysis methods of the statistical sufficiency of observations (module `analysis.sufficiency`): `estimate_sampling_error`, the error of estimating the mean of a field over a period from each target's samples, for a field with a standard deviation and an exponential correlation in time (exact, see `sampling_error_variance`), with the effective number of independent samples; `sample_field`, which samples a field (an xarray `DataArray`, such as a nature run) at the time and place of each observation (without a value outside the field's period or, for nearest values, its extent, as of a regional nature run), and `compare_sampled_means`, which measures the sampling error against it; `compute_revisit_statistics`, the quantiles of revisits and the longest gap of each target (including the edges of the period); and `compute_local_time_coverage`, the distribution of samples in local time of day. Each observation is a sample, or, with `merge_overlapping`, the overlapping observations of a target (as by several platforms at once) are one sample, as `aggregate_observations` merges them, so that `samples` counts the same looks as `reduce_observations`; with `sample_interval`, an observation longer than the interval (as a fixed platform's continuous view of a target) is sampled every interval within its period; the time of an observation is its `epoch`, `time`, or `closest_time`, or else the midpoint of its `start` and `end`, unless `time_column` names another. The decorrelation time of `estimate_sampling_error` may differ between targets. The validation notebook `ValidateSamplingErrorIMERG` compares its predictions with the errors of monthly means of IMERG precipitation sampled by four constellation designs, and the example notebook `AssessSamplingSufficiency` shows the qualities of the methods with a synthetic nature run. `estimate_sampling_error` and `compute_revisit_statistics` compute the statistics of every target at once (the sum of the correlations between pairs of samples by a recursion over their times, and the gaps and their quantiles from the samples in order), with the same results 3 to 12 times faster.
 - Module `tradespace` (prototype) for trade studies. A `DesignSpace` declares design variables (`Real`, `Integer`, `Categorical`), fixed and `Derived` values, and `Uncertain` parameters (drawn per replicate, not searched), grouped hierarchically with `Choice` (one of several branches) and `Repeat` (a variable number of items, such as orbit planes, optionally unordered). Bounds, derived values, and constraints are callables of the values decided before them. `DesignSpace.enumerate` gives every feasible design (a full factorial design, including variables that exist only in some designs), and `sample` a Latin hypercube or random sample. A `Design` has a `key` from its decisions and `flat` values by path, and `realize` draws a replicate's uncertain parameters, with common random numbers between designs by default. `Study` builds and evaluates designs in replicates into a data frame, in worker processes with `n_jobs` (or on an `executor`; idle workers wait two minutes for more evaluations before they exit), giving the realized design to an evaluation function with a parameter named `design`, recording as infeasible the designs that its functions reject with `Infeasible` or that fail the validation of TAT-C's schemas (other errors, including other `ValueError`s, are raised or recorded as failed), and raising an error for metrics that would replace its status columns or a design's values; `summarize` gives the mean, standard deviation, and standard error of metrics over replicates.
 - Class `tradespace.SatelliteCache`, which caches an analysis of single satellites by their content (orbit, instruments, and attitude, not their name, see `get_satellite_key`), in memory or on disk, so that designs that share satellites compute their results once. On disk, results are kept by namespace and by the definition of the function (see `get_definition`); a cache warns if its namespace has results only of its function with other code, naming what differs, `SatelliteCache.prune` removes the results of other definitions, and results that cannot be read are computed again.
 - Functions `tradespace.pareto_front`, `pareto_ranks` (both with optional tolerances of objectives, within which designs near the front are efficient), and `hypervolume` (exact) of designs with several objectives to minimize or maximize, and `pareto_probability`, the probability that each design is efficient given the uncertainty of its replicates (a bootstrap that resamples the same replicates for every design).
 - Criteria by which designs are judged (`tradespace.Criteria`): objectives to minimize or maximize, requirements (`Requirement`, output constraints that designs meet or violate once evaluated, by an amount relative to the required value or a `scale`), the statistics over replicates that they name (`Mean`, `StandardDeviation`, `Minimum`, `Maximum`, `Quantile`, and `Probability`, the share of replicates that meet a threshold; other names are means of metrics), tolerances of objectives, and the columns that group the records of an evaluation (`by`, such as a sensor), whose designs are judged separately. `summarize` gives statistics of each design (and, with criteria, the violations of their requirements), with the standard deviations and standard errors of means. `pareto_front`, `pareto_ranks` (which ranks designs that miss requirements after those that meet them), `hypervolume`, `pareto_probability` (which also gives the probability of meeting the requirements), `refine`, `confirm`, `screen`, `optimize_optuna` (a constraint of each trial for each requirement), and `optimize_pymoo` (a constraint for each requirement) take criteria, or a mapping of objectives to their directions. Results with several records of an evaluation that `by` does not separate raise an error.
 - Function `tradespace.refine` (and class `Refinement`), which adds replicates only to the designs whose Pareto efficiency is uncertain, until it is settled, optionally with a tolerance of differences that do not matter (also of `pareto_probability`), and within a `budget` of evaluations (refining the least settled designs first). Designs about a tolerance from the front, which more replicates settle only slowly, are `indifferent` and not refined once their objectives are known to within a share of their tolerances (`precision`, from the standard errors of the objectives that `pareto_probability` gives). `Study.run` evaluates given replicates (`replicate_indices`).
 - Class `tradespace.ResultStore`, a persistent store (SQLite) of the evaluations of a study (argument `store` of `Study`), from which a study that is interrupted or extended takes the evaluations stored before (except evaluations that failed and designs that failed validation, which are evaluated again). Workers add each evaluation as it completes, so that an interrupted run loses only the evaluations in progress, and records are kept as JSON (with values such as dates and infinite numbers tagged, so that they are read exactly). Function `tradespace.sensitivity` (and `main_effects`), the share of the variance of metrics explained by each design variable where it is active. Tolerances of objectives may be percentages of their values (such as `"5%"`). `Uncertain.seed` draws a seed of random numbers for each replicate, and `remove_failures` removes the satellites that fail, the same in every design.
 - Building blocks of constellations: fragments of design spaces of Walker constellations (`tradespace.walker_space`) and planes of sun-synchronous orbits (`sso_planes_space`, whose planes have different crossing times), `build_constellation`, which builds the satellites of either with instruments or entries of the instrument catalog (with their validated attitudes, several entries on one satellite if they share an attitude, the `start` and `end` of the analysis for an attitude scheduled by day, and the `grade` and `platform` by which configurations are selected), and `count_satellites` and `count_planes`. The phase and node of the lead satellite of a Walker constellation (`phase_fraction` and `node_fraction`, design variables or uncertain parameters) are fractions of the intervals over which they give distinct constellations (`360 / per_plane` degrees of phase, and `360 / planes` degrees of node for a delta configuration or 360 for a star), so that a search does not evaluate the same constellation at several phases or nodes. Planes of inclined orbits (`planes_space`), each with its own inclination and node (uncertain by default, as of satellites launched as secondary payloads). Streets-of-coverage constellations (`soc_space`), whose planes and satellites in each plane the fragment derives from a swath width, inclination, and altitude, and mutual orbiting groups (`mog_space`), launched together as one plane. The fragments name the number of planes `planes` and the satellites in each plane `per_plane`. The satellites of the planes of `sso_planes_space` and `planes_space` are evenly spaced, or follow each other in a train at an `interval` (minutes), on the same ground track or in the same plane (`repeat_ground_track`). The phase of each plane (`phase_fraction`) is a fraction of the interval over which it gives distinct planes (`360 / per_plane` degrees for evenly spaced satellites, 360 for a train), from which each fragment derives the plane's `true_anomaly`, as for the lead satellite of a Walker constellation. `build_constellation`, `count_satellites`, and `count_planes` take several layouts (a list, or a `Repeat` of a `Choice` of layouts), such as the shells of a mixed constellation. The values of each fragment in a design are those of a `Layout` (`layout_of`, with `WalkerLayout`, `SSOPlanesLayout`, `PlanesLayout`, `SOCLayout`, `MOGLayout`, and `Layouts` of several), which gives its `Plane`s and the orbits of their satellites in order; other layouts subclass it and are built, counted (`plane_sizes`), deployed, and costed as the others.
 - Function `tradespace.screen` (and class `Screening`), which screens designs with studies of increasing fidelity, keeping whole fronts of the best designs of each stage for the next.
 - Searches of design spaces with optional optimizers: `tradespace.optimize_optuna` suggests designs with Optuna trials (define-by-run over the hierarchical space), and `optimize_pymoo` and `make_pymoo_problem` search the unit hypercube that encodes a design space (a dimension per decision) with pymoo, whose algorithms eliminate the proposals of designs proposed or evaluated before (`distinct`, by design, `make_pymoo_problem`'s `duplicates`). Each design is evaluated once, and the designs proposed together are evaluated together, in parallel if the study is. A search returns its results and designs (`Search`), which `refine` and `confirm` refine and confirm as for any study. Constraints of a design space may return the amount of their violation (`Infeasible.violation`), which Optuna's trials record (`set_constraint`) for its constraint-aware samplers, and which is the constraint value of pymoo problems. Optional dependencies `tradespace` (joblib, Optuna, and pymoo).
 - Deployment of constellations (`tradespace.DeploymentSchedule`): from the rates and durations of a study (the mission, the design life, development, and production rate of each type of satellite, its production on a line, and launches at a cadence and capacity, in number or mass, optionally in an order of planes), each design's deployment (`DeploymentSchedule.plan`, a `Deployment` of its `Production`, `Launch`es, and replacements), so that a design of more satellites and planes takes longer to deploy. A sustained constellation (`DeploymentSchedule.sustained`, such as of an operational program over the period of a study) is replaced after the end of the mission as before it, and its costs are those incurred by the end, which count the units in production then by the share produced and later blocks by the share of their development done. A deployment's launches divide the mission into phases of constant sets of operating satellites (`Deployment.phases`), evaluated over the study's representative period from the observations of the full constellation (`evaluate_phases`) and combined over the mission (`summarize_phases`: the time-weighted mean of each metric and the time until it first meets a requirement).
 - Simulated missions with failures (`Deployment.simulate`, a `MissionHistory` for each replicate, drawn from its seed with common random numbers between designs): lives of satellites whose failure rates vary over the lifecycle (`tradespace.Exponential`, `Weibull`, `Bathtub` of competing causes, and `Piecewise` rates by age, `Lifetime`), failures of launches (`FailureModel`), and a policy that degrades or restores failed satellites (`RestorationPolicy`) by spares in orbit (taking a failed satellite's place after drifting), spares on the ground (launched at the next launch slot), or satellites produced when needed (after procurement, on the production line), optionally replenishing spares and batching restorations in launches. A history gives the phases in which satellites operate (`MissionHistory.phases`), measures of the mission (`MissionHistory.metrics`: failures, restorations, spares used, time to restore, and availability), and, priced by a `CostModel`, the cost of what it produces and launches. Policies are decisions of designs with a fragment of a design space (`policy_space`: whether to restore, spares in orbit and on the ground, and other fields of the policy, without spares that would never be used), and `configure` makes a policy (or a deployment schedule, or another dataclass) from the values of a design. Many histories of a replicate's mission (`Deployment.simulate_many`) share its sample of targets: `evaluate_histories` evaluates them (with their measures and costs) with a cache that evaluates each set of operating satellites once (`PhaseCache`, keyed by the content of the satellites and shared by the histories, policies, and designs of a replicate), and `summarize_histories` gives their means, quantiles (the risk of the mission), and probabilities of meeting requirements, as one record of the replicate. The histories of a replicate are stratified by default (a Latin hypercube over the histories of each satellite's life and each launch, drawn from uniform numbers by `Lifetime.from_uniform`), which estimates the means of missions 3 to 15 times more precisely than independent histories, and means may be estimated with control variates (`summarize_histories(controls=...)`, such as the availability of the mission, whose expected value simulations alone estimate). `allocate_histories` recommends the histories of each replicate (and the replicates, for a standard error) from a pilot with independent histories, by the variances between replicates and between histories and their costs (from the `StandardDeviation` of a metric over the histories). `exact_expectations` computes the expected capability and availability of a mission without restoration exactly (the sets of operating satellites by their probabilities, from the survival of each launch group, with planned replacements), without sampling error. Histories may be drawn from stressed failures for importance sampling (`simulate_many(stress=...)`: hazards and launch failures times a factor, each history weighted by its likelihood ratio censored at the end of the mission, `Lifetime.stressed` and `MissionHistory.weight`), which `summarize_histories` weighs in every statistic, with the effective number of histories: the probabilities of rare losses with 30 to 70 times less variance.
 - Life-cycle costs of deployments for trade studies (`tradespace.CostModel`): the development of each type of satellite, the production of its units on a learning curve (`SatelliteCost`), launches (per launch, or prices per satellite and kg, `LaunchCost`), operations (`OperationsCost`), replacements, and spares in orbit, dated by the deployment for their present value at a discount rate (`CostEstimate.present_value`, metric `cost present`) and annual costs (`CostEstimate.annual`, metric `cost peak annual`), with growth factors for cost risk (such as uncertain parameters, whose quantiles over replicates give the cost at a confidence level). TAT-C does not include cost-estimating relationships: the costs are given by the study. Fingerprints describe dataclasses (such as cost models) by their fields.
 - Function `tradespace.confirm` (and class `Confirmation`), which evaluates selected designs (such as a front) with new replicates that played no part in their selection, removing the optimism of the selection (the winner's curse, `Confirmation.optimism`). `ResultStore` keys evaluations by the fingerprint of a study (`Study.fingerprint`, a hash of `Study.definition`, see `get_definition`: its seed, use of common random numbers, design space, and build and evaluation functions, with the data frames they are given described by their content, the version of Python, and the source of the modules of TAT-C and resource files they depend on, found from their imports) and keeps each definition, so that variants of a study share a store without mixing their evaluations, and a study warns of evaluations of the same study with other code (of its functions, of TAT-C, or of Python), which are not reused, naming what differs (`fingerprint.compare_definitions`); `Study.prune` removes them (and not the evaluations of other studies that share the store). Changes to TAT-C that a study does not depend on (such as to modules it does not use) keep its results. Namespaces of stores and caches are optional, for what fingerprints cannot see (data that functions read from files, and the code of other modules). `refine` treats designs with fewer than `min_replicates` (5) as uncertain whatever their probability, adds the replicates left to `max_replicates` in a last partial step, and warns if designs remain uncertain without a tolerance. `sensitivity` gives adjusted shares (epsilon squared) by default, without the share that the levels of a variable explain by chance (`adjusted=False` for correlation ratios).
 - The schedule and cost model of a `tradespace.MissionPlan` may be functions of the design, which size them by its values with relationships of the study's own, such as the costs of a type of satellite from its payload or the capacity of launches to the design's altitude; launches limited in mass take the masses of the satellites from the plan (`masses`). The `launch_mass_capacity` of a `DeploymentSchedule` may be a function of a plane, for planes at different inclinations.
 - The start of the period of `tradespace.Observations` may be a value of the design (`start_of` and `end_of` give a design's period), such as an uncertain epoch drawn for each replicate over the mission, whose observations are cached apart, so that replicates average over the geometry and seasons of the mission that the one window of quasi-static phases misses (see `evaluate_phases`). The schedule of deployments states that a design life does not retire a satellite that is not replaced, and simulations that a planned replacement whose launch fails is not flown again.
 - Utilities of the metrics of designs (`tradespace.Utility`), for trade spaces of utility against cost: a single-attribute utility of each metric (`LinearUtility`, `ExponentialUtility` of a risk attitude, `PiecewiseUtility`, or any function), combined by weights additively or in Keeney and Raiffa's multiplicative form. A utility is a metric of any statistic (its `Mean` is the expected utility over the replicates, and a utility given as a statistic of `Criteria` is its mean), computed in each replicate, and gives the utility of each row of a data frame (such as a summary of the means of designs). Statistics name the columns they need (`Statistic.columns`).
 - Staged deployments (`tradespace.Stage`, argument `stages` of `DeploymentSchedule.plan` and `MissionPlan`, which may give the stages of each design): planes that are produced and launched only if a rule decides to, at times during the mission, from what it knows then (`Decision`: the satellites operating, the history so far, and, through the design, its uncertain parameters and scenario), after a lead time and at the next launch slots, and replaced and restored as other planes. Rules may also read the capability of the satellites operating at a decision (`Decision.capability`, the study's metrics of their observations, as of a phase), which the evaluations of an `ObservationStudy` give each deployment (`Deployment.capability`, a `tradespace.Capability` of its satellites, observations, and cached phases, which also caches the phases of studies without failures), such as for a plane deployed only if those in orbit fall short. A stage that is not deployed is neither produced, launched, nor priced; `MissionHistory.decided` gives when each was deployed, and its metrics the `stages deployed`. Exact expectations of missions without restoration (`exact_expectations`) need deployments that are not staged, so that studies of staged deployments sample their histories.
 - Scenarios of studies (argument `scenarios` of `tradespace.Study`): conditions under which designs are compared rather than averaged over, each giving fixed values or uncertain parameters of the design space other values (`Design.realize` takes a `scenario`), so that each design is evaluated in each scenario (recorded as `scenario`, and judged in each with criteria `by` it). Each scenario's evaluations are stored by its values, so that a scenario added later is evaluated alone. Function `tradespace.robustness` gives each design's Pareto trace (the share of scenarios in which it is efficient) and its regret in each scenario (the improvement in every objective it would need to match the scenario's front) and the largest. The metrics of an `ObservationStudy` (and of `PhaseCache`, `evaluate_histories`, and `exact_expectations`) are given the realized design if they have a parameter named `design`.
 - Criteria across the scenarios of a study (`tradespace.Across`, argument `across` of `Criteria`), which judge each design once by its worst or its (weighted) mean statistics over the scenarios (or the groups of another column), so that searches find designs robust to scenarios; a requirement may be met in every scenario while objectives are means.
 - Function `tradespace.attribute_variance` (and class `Attribution`), which attributes the variance of metrics between the replicates of each design to its uncertain parameters (or groups of them): the share that each causes, alone or with others, and the share that knowing it would remove, with standard errors, by evaluating designs with one parameter drawn as in other replicates and the others held (which works for seeds, whose values do not order outcomes); `Attribution.summary` pools the shares of several designs, and `Attribution.differences` attributes the variance of the differences between pairs of designs, from the same evaluations (parameters that act alike on both cancel, with common random numbers). `Design.realize` and `Study.run` take a `shift`, which draws named parameters as in other replicates (kept apart in a `ResultStore` as a variant of the replicates), and `DesignSpace.uncertain_paths` lists the uncertain parameters.
 - Structured cost risk (after Maier, Yeakel, and Wendoloski, "Cost Variance Analysis in Constellation Architecture Studies," 2023): growth factors of each type of satellite (by kind, for development, production, and launches, in `CostModel.estimate` and `MissionPlan.growth`), and the costs of types of satellites as sums of their components (`tradespace.Component`, `SatelliteCost.components`), whose growth has a distribution by maturity (such as `tradespace.Triangular`), correlated between components (`CostModel.correlation`). `CostModel.draw` draws the growth of the components from standard normal scores of the components and a common score, which `tradespace.risk_space` declares as uncertain parameters of a design space (named by `MissionPlan.scores`, also of a nested group), so that common random numbers give the designs that share a component the same growth of it; without drawn growth, components cost their expected growth. Types of satellites that share a component (by name) develop it once in a deployment. Production in blocks (`SatelliteCost.block_size`): each block after the first develops its type again (a share of its development, `redevelopment`) before its first unit is produced, and restarts the learning curve; `CostEstimate.blocks` counts the blocks of each type. Function `tradespace.exceedance` gives the probability that a metric of each design exceeds that of each other in the same replicate (such as that one design costs more than another), and `plot_exceedance` plots them as a matrix. The validation notebook `ValidateCostVarianceNSOSA` reproduces the paper's example of two NSOSA constellations that share a configuration.
 - Searches with Optuna accept categorical options that depend on earlier decisions (a parameter for each set of options), use TPE with a constant liar in batches by default (which spreads the trials of a batch), and stop after `max_designs` designs evaluated (trials that suggest designs evaluated before are told their objectives without evaluating them again). A design infeasible in some of its replicates violates its feasibility by their share (`Evaluations.infeasibility`), and the first design evaluated in a search is evaluated alone, to check that evaluations give one record each. `Study` records errors of evaluations as infeasible, without storing them, with `errors="record"`. Enumeration walks the items of unordered repetitions in one order only, and `DesignSpace.size` counts designs without keeping them. Pareto fronts are found by a vectorized sweep in lexicographic order that compares designs only with the efficient designs before them, and fronts with tolerances by comparing designs only with the efficient designs without tolerances (which dominate every design that any design dominates within the tolerances), in blocks of bounded memory.

 - `GeneralPerturbationsOrbit` propagates orbit tracks and observation events consistently, as selected by its `repeat_cycle`: if set, times before the first and after the last element's epoch are propagated with that element maintained on its repeat ground track; other times use the closest element. Orbits are now propagated directly with SGP4 by default; use `remove_drag=True` and `repeat_cycle="auto"` for the former default. Removed the runtime settings `repeat_cycle_for_orbit_track` and `repeat_cycle_for_observation_events`, the `try_repeat` arguments, and methods `get_observation_repeat_cycle`, `get_repeat_shifts`, and `get_repeat_orbit_track`. A warning is issued when elements with drag are propagated far from their epoch.

Cached repeat cycles are recomputed when the search options change. Elements with a perigee below the Earth's surface (for example, from a mean motion in revolutions per day rather than radians per minute) have no repeat cycle, with a warning, rather than a search lasting minutes.

Analysis time windows must have timezone-aware `start` and `end` datetimes with `end` no earlier than `start` (a `ValueError`), and an `instrument_index` out of range raises an `IndexError` naming the satellite.
 - `get_observation_events` refines rise and set times to a millisecond, and finds passes that culminate outside the search period or span a switch between elements.

 - `collect_observations` refines access periods to the instrument's field of regard, uses the time the view sweeps over the point as the epoch of a `PointedInstrument` observation, and evaluates orbits with a repeat cycle consistently.
 - `collect_observations` and `collect_region_observations` accept one or more points or regions and one or more satellites, and `instrument_index=None` for every instrument of each satellite, computing all of their observations together (concatenated and sorted by start). `collect_multi_observations` and `collect_multi_region_observations` are kept as wrappers for backwards compatibility.
 - `collect_observations` computes the observations of all points by each satellite and instrument together, vectorized across the points: the orbit is propagated once to screen the windows in which each point may be observed, and the periods, epochs, and validity of the observations are refined in all windows at once (in blocks that bound memory). Many points are about 50 times faster to collect (for example, 649 points, 24 satellites, and one day, from about 220 to 4.5 seconds), and a point's observations do not depend on the other points (to within a microsecond). Observation times are always in nanoseconds (`datetime64[ns, UTC]`), as are those of `collect_downlinks`.
 - `collect_orbit_track`, `collect_ground_track`, `collect_ground_pixels`, `collect_limb_observations`, and `collect_downlinks` accept one or more satellites (and the track methods `instrument_index=None` for every instrument), and `collect_ro_observations` one or more receivers, with the results of several concatenated and sorted by time (or start). Downlinks and RO observations of several satellites are computed together.
 - `PointedInstrument` roll and pitch angles rotate the view rigidly (roll, then pitch about the rolled cross-track axis), and `compute_view_tangents` accepts them. Documented the pointing conventions (positive roll looks left, positive pitch looks forward, and cross-track pixel indices run from right to left).
 - `compute_latencies` downlinks an observation that ends during a downlink at the end of that downlink by default (`during_contact="end"`; formerly `"next"`).
 - `PointedInstrument.cross_track_oversampling` and `along_track_oversampling` may be negative, for gaps between pixels (as of an undersampling sounder).
 - `collect_ro_observations` uses the point of minimum WGS 84 geodetic altitude on the receiver-transmitter line as the tangent point.
 - `SOCConstellation` uses the polar streets-of-coverage pattern for near-polar orbits (new field `polar` and methods `is_polar`, `get_footprint_angle`, `get_polar_design`, `get_satellites_per_plane`, and `get_number_planes`; `generate_walker` raises a `ValueError` for polar designs) and a continuously covering hexagonal lattice for inclined orbits, which requires more satellites at a given packing distance.
 - `collect_ground_track` and `collect_ground_pixels` cull times with a mask using the periods when the instrument's field of regard may observe it, which no longer misses footprints near the poles or across the anti-meridian. Masks are split along the anti-meridian and poles before use, and results are sorted by time.
 - Observations of points (`collect_observations`, `collect_multi_observations`, and `compute_latencies`) identify each point by the hash of its geometry (`target_hash`, see `hash_geometry`) rather than a `point_id`, and `aggregate_observations`, `reduce_observations`, and `reduce_latencies` group observations by `target_hash`. The `id` of a TAT-C `Point` is no longer used.
 - Generated points and cells are identified by the hashes of their geometries (`point_id` and `cell_id`) rather than integer indices, so that a generated point's `point_id` equals the `target_hash` of its observations.
 - Generating points and cells is about 3 to 15 times faster (vectorized geometry construction, hashing, and clipping). Points and cells clipped to a mask keep the grid or lattice order rather than the arbitrary order of `geopandas.clip`.

 - Fixed rectangular footprints of wide, elongated views.
 - Fixed `PointedInstrument.compute_footprint_pixel_array` for a single-time (scalar) orbit track.
 - Fixed `collect_observations` missing short observations (and misplacing the ends of long ones) when a field of regard extends to the horizon of a long access period, as near the perigee of a Molniya orbit, which were refined from too few samples of the access period.
 - Fixed the pixel positions of a `PointedInstrument` with oversampling (`compute_projected_pixel_position`, `compute_footprint_pixel_array`, and `collect_ground_pixels`), which spaced the pixels by a fraction of the field of view rather than across all of it.
 - Fixed `split_polygon` for polygons around a pole whose boundary is not monotonic in longitude (such as Antarctica's coastline), which it reconstructed by sorting their vertices by longitude, removing and adding strips of the polygon: they are now reconstructed in their vertex order, unrolled across the anti-meridian.

 - Fixed `compute_ground_track` to identify orbits with the period of the element that propagates the orbit (as selected by `element_switch_times`), rather than the element whose epoch is closest.
 - Fixed `collect_ro_observations` labeling some occultations of transmitters nearly abeam of the receiver (yaw angles near 90 degrees) as setting rather than rising: `is_rising` follows the direction of the tangent point through `sample_elevation`. `collect_ro_observations` also splits a grazing occultation, whose tangent point descends and rises again, into a setting and a rising observation where the tangent point turns, so that each observation crosses `sample_elevation` at most once.
 - Fixed `compute_latencies` for observations and downlinks whose times have different units (for example, microseconds and nanoseconds).
 - Fixed `split_polygon` for longitudes beyond 180 degrees, for polygons spanning -180 to 180 degrees of longitude, and for holes across the anti-meridian or around a pole (holes are now split like exteriors), and to drop degenerate (zero-area) parts left by repairing invalid polygons.
