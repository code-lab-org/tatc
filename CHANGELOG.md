# TAT-C Change Log

## Unreleased

Added a catalog of instruments from past and present missions, with a standard for validating them. Fixed instruments losing their fields when serialized. Instruments now reject unknown fields, which can break code that passes misspelled or unused arguments.

Added:
 - Module `catalog` of instruments from past and present missions (`get_entry`, `list_entries`), starting with GOES-R ABI, GPM GMI, GCOM-W AMSR2, ATMS, CrIS, VIIRS, MODIS, Landsat OLI, Sentinel-2 MSI, Sentinel-1 C-SAR, NISAR L-SAR, SWOT KaRIn, the SMAP radiometer, IASI, Sentinel-5P TROPOMI, GPM DPR, MHS, ICESat-2 ATLAS, AIRS, AMSU-A, AVHRR, SSMIS, Sentinel-3 OLCI, PACE OCI, HARP2, and SPEXone, the OMPS Nadir Mapper, Himawari AHI, CERES, ECOSTRESS, OCO-2, the Sentinel-6 Poseidon-4 altimeter, Metop ASCAT, TEMPO, GEDI, and EMIT. `InstrumentEntry.to_satellite` and `build` give an entry's instruments with the attitude they were validated with, and `InstrumentEntry.validation` gives its validation tier, computed from its results and the thresholds of the validation standard (`docs/validation/standard.rst`). GMI, ATMS, VIIRS, MODIS, OLI, MSI, KaRIn, TROPOMI, DPR, MHS, HARP2, SPEXone, Poseidon-4, ASCAT, and TEMPO are `validated`, each with stated limitations, and ABI, AMSR2, CrIS, C-SAR, L-SAR, the SMAP radiometer, IASI, ATLAS, AIRS, AMSU-A, AVHRR, SSMIS, OLCI, OCI, OMPS, AHI, CERES, ECOSTRESS, OCO-2, GEDI, and EMIT are `partial` and list their gaps. Results on sibling satellites (NOAA-21, Metop-B, Sentinel-1D, Sentinel-6B) are recorded as transfers, which warn on use and do not count toward a tier; ATMS and VIIRS also have configurations fitted on NOAA-21, validated on held-out data, as NOAA-20's angles did not transfer. In an entry with configurations fitted on more than one platform (ATMS, VIIRS, and the OMPS Nadir Mapper), each configuration's name ends with its platform (`swath_noaa_20`, `swath_noaa_21`). Version 2 of the standard adds a lidar class, for ATLAS, version 3 an altimeter class, for Poseidon-4, version 4 a scatterometer class, for ASCAT, with the gates of the SAR class, and version 5 a regional geostationary class, for TEMPO, whose gate is the intersection over union of the field of regard with the area a scan images; none changes a threshold of an earlier version. Every pointed configuration with a view tailored to the time step is swept (`is_swept`), so its swaths do not depend on the time step; its records were rescored with swept swaths on the same held-out data, and MSI, TROPOMI, DPR, OCI's swath, and C-SAR's EW mode, whose tiers changed, were tested again on a later held-out day, with the rescored results stated in their limitations. The documentation lists the entries and their results on a page written from the catalog (`docs/validation/catalog_page.py`), and the example `UseInstrumentCatalog` shows their use. The OSSE examples `RunOSSEMonsoon` and `RunOSSEHurricane` take their ATMS and CrIS swaths from the catalog.
 - Class `catalog.TiltSchedule` and field `attitude_schedule` of catalog configurations, for an attitude that changes by day, as PACE's tilt does: `build` and `to_satellite` take the `start` and `end` of the analysis and give an `AttitudeSchedule` with an attitude for each day (UTC), blind while the tilt changes. Function `catalog.get_subsolar_latitude` gives the latitude of the subsolar point.
 - Field `type` of a catalog configuration's `attitude` (`attitude`, `glint`, or `target`), which builds an `Attitude`, `GlintAttitude`, or `TargetAttitude`, as for OCO-2's glint mode.
 - Field `blind_intervals` of `Attitude`: intervals of argument of latitude in which a space system's instruments do not observe, for example while the body or a mechanism moves. Observations in them are not valid, so point observations, ground tracks (`valid_obs`, and the swaths of `compute_ground_track`), orbit tracks, and ground pixels omit them, and the periods and swaths of region observations end at them. Method `is_observing` of attitudes, schedules, and instruments.
 - Field `is_swept` of `PointedInstrument`, for an instrument whose view sweeps along track: its footprint over a time step is the strip its cross-track line (the directions at an along-track angle of zero, which `collect_observations` checks) sweeps between the step's bounds, trimmed to blind intervals, so that ground track swaths (`compute_ground_track`) and region swaths do not depend on the time step, while its along-track field of view still sets the instantaneous view. Method `PointedInstrument.compute_swept_footprints`, utility method `compute_swept_footprints`, argument `time_step` of `collect_ground_track` and `compute_ground_track` (the span of each footprint, by default half the gap to the neighboring times on each side), and runtime configuration `swept_footprint_max_substep`. The default, `False`, leaves footprints unchanged.
 - Field `type` (`instrument`, `pointed`, or `conical`) of `Instrument`, `PointedInstrument`, and `ConicalInstrument`, which identifies the instrument class in serialized data. Data without a `type` loads as the class its fields imply.
 - Function `generation.generate_points_rotated_lattice`, the points of a Fibonacci lattice in a random orientation: a stratified sample of the sphere (or of a mask), each point uniform by area, whose statistics have about 1.5 to 4 times less variance between replicates of trade studies than those of random points.
 - Analysis methods of the statistical sufficiency of observations (module `analysis.sufficiency`): `estimate_sampling_error`, the error of estimating the mean of a field over a period from each target's samples, for a field with a standard deviation and an exponential correlation in time (exact, see `sampling_error_variance`), with the effective number of independent samples; `sample_field`, which samples a field (an xarray `DataArray`, such as a nature run) at the time and place of each observation, and `compare_sampled_means`, which measures the sampling error against it; `compute_revisit_statistics`, the quantiles of revisits and the longest gap of each target (including the edges of the period); and `compute_local_time_coverage`, the distribution of samples in local time of day. The decorrelation time of `estimate_sampling_error` may differ between targets. The validation notebook `ValidateSamplingErrorIMERG` compares its predictions with the errors of monthly means of IMERG precipitation sampled by four constellation designs. `estimate_sampling_error` and `compute_revisit_statistics` compute the statistics of every target at once (the sum of the correlations between pairs of samples by a recursion over their times, and the gaps and their quantiles from the samples in order), with the same results 3 to 12 times faster.
 - Module `tradespace` (prototype) for trade studies. A `DesignSpace` declares design variables (`Real`, `Integer`, `Categorical`), fixed and `Derived` values, and `Uncertain` parameters (drawn per replicate, not searched), grouped hierarchically with `Choice` (one of several branches) and `Repeat` (a variable number of items, such as orbit planes, optionally unordered). Bounds, derived values, and constraints are callables of the values decided before them. `DesignSpace.enumerate` gives every feasible design (a full factorial design, including variables that exist only in some designs), and `sample` a Latin hypercube or random sample. A `Design` has a `key` from its decisions and `flat` values by path, and `realize` draws a replicate's uncertain parameters, with common random numbers between designs by default. `Study` builds and evaluates designs in replicates into a data frame, in worker processes with `n_jobs` (or on an `executor`; idle workers wait two minutes for more evaluations before they exit), giving the realized design to an evaluation function with a parameter named `design`, recording as infeasible the designs that its functions reject with `Infeasible` or that fail the validation of TAT-C's schemas (other errors, including other `ValueError`s, are raised or recorded as failed), and raising an error for metrics that would replace its status columns or a design's values; `summarize` gives the mean, standard deviation, and standard error of metrics over replicates.
 - Class `tradespace.SatelliteCache`, which caches an analysis of single satellites by their content (orbit, instruments, and attitude, not their name, see `get_satellite_key`), in memory or on disk, so that designs that share satellites compute their results once. On disk, results are kept by namespace and by the definition of the function (see `get_definition`); a cache warns if its namespace has results only of other definitions, naming what differs, `SatelliteCache.prune` removes them, and results that cannot be read are computed again.
 - Functions `tradespace.pareto_front`, `pareto_ranks` (both with optional tolerances of objectives, within which designs near the front are efficient), and `hypervolume` (exact) of designs with several objectives to minimize or maximize, and `pareto_probability`, the probability that each design is efficient given the uncertainty of its replicates (a bootstrap that resamples the same replicates for every design).
 - Criteria by which designs are judged (`tradespace.Criteria`): objectives to minimize or maximize, requirements (`Requirement`, output constraints that designs meet or violate once evaluated, by an amount relative to the required value or a `scale`), the statistics over replicates that they name (`Mean`, `StandardDeviation`, `StandardError`, `Minimum`, `Maximum`, `Quantile`, and `Probability`, the share of replicates that meet a threshold; other names are means of metrics), tolerances of objectives, and the columns that group the records of an evaluation (`by`, such as a sensor), whose designs are judged separately. `aggregate` gives statistics of each design (and, with criteria, the violations of their requirements), and `summarize` means with their standard deviations and standard errors. `pareto_front`, `pareto_ranks` (which ranks designs that miss requirements after those that meet them), `hypervolume`, `pareto_probability` (which also gives the probability of meeting the requirements), `refine`, `confirm`, `screen`, `optimize_optuna` (a constraint of each trial for each requirement), and `optimize_pymoo` (a constraint for each requirement) take criteria, or a mapping of objectives to their directions. Results with several records of an evaluation that `by` does not separate raise an error rather than being averaged.
 - Function `tradespace.refine` (and class `Refinement`), which adds replicates only to the designs whose Pareto efficiency is uncertain, until it is settled, optionally with a tolerance of differences that do not matter (also of `pareto_probability`), and within a `budget` of evaluations (refining the least settled designs first). Designs about a tolerance from the front, which more replicates settle only slowly, are `indifferent` and not refined once their objectives are known to within a share of their tolerances (`precision`, from the standard errors of the objectives that `pareto_probability` gives). `Study.run` evaluates given replicates (`replicates`).
 - Class `tradespace.ResultStore`, a persistent store (SQLite) of the evaluations of a study (argument `store` of `Study`), from which a study that is interrupted or extended takes the evaluations stored before (except evaluations that failed and designs that failed validation, which are evaluated again). Workers add each evaluation as it completes, so that an interrupted run loses only the evaluations in progress, and records are kept as JSON (with values such as dates and infinite numbers tagged, so that they are read exactly). Function `tradespace.sensitivity` (and `main_effects`), the share of the variance of metrics explained by each design variable where it is active. Tolerances of objectives may be percentages of their values (such as `"5%"`). `Uncertain.seed` draws a seed of random numbers for each replicate, and `remove_failures` removes the satellites that fail, the same in every design.
 - Building blocks of constellations: fragments of design spaces of Walker constellations (`tradespace.walker_space`) and planes of sun-synchronous orbits (`sso_planes_space`, whose planes have different crossing times), `build_constellation`, which builds the satellites of either with instruments or entries of the instrument catalog (with their validated attitudes, and several entries on one satellite), and `count_satellites` and `count_planes`. The phase and node of the lead satellite of a Walker constellation (`phase_fraction` and `node_fraction`, design variables or uncertain parameters) are fractions of the intervals over which they give distinct constellations (`360 / per_plane` degrees of phase, and `360 / planes` degrees of node for a delta configuration or 360 for a star), so that a search does not evaluate the same constellation at several phases or nodes. Planes of inclined orbits (`planes_space`), each with its own inclination and node (uncertain by default, as of satellites launched as secondary payloads). The satellites of the planes of `sso_planes_space` and `planes_space` are evenly spaced, or follow each other in a train at an `interval` (minutes), on the same ground track or in the same plane (`repeat_ground_track`). The phase of each plane (`phase_fraction`, which replaces `phase` of `sso_planes_space`) is a fraction of the interval over which it gives distinct planes (`360 / satellites` degrees for evenly spaced satellites, 360 for a train), from which each fragment derives the plane's `true_anomaly`, as for the lead satellite of a Walker constellation. `build_constellation`, `count_satellites`, and `count_planes` take several layouts (a list, or a `Repeat` of a `Choice` of layouts), such as the shells of a mixed constellation. The values of each fragment in a design are those of a `Layout` (`layout_of`, with `WalkerLayout`, `SSOPlanesLayout`, `PlanesLayout`, and `Layouts` of several), which gives its `Plane`s and the orbits of their satellites in order; other layouts subclass it and are built, counted (`plane_sizes`), deployed, and costed as the others.
 - Function `tradespace.screen` (and class `Screening`), which screens designs with studies of increasing fidelity, keeping whole fronts of the best designs of each stage for the next.
 - Searches of design spaces with optional optimizers: `tradespace.optimize_optuna` suggests designs with Optuna trials (`DesignSpace.suggest`, define-by-run over the hierarchical space), and `optimize_pymoo` and `make_pymoo_problem` search the unit hypercube that encodes a design space (`DesignSpace.unit_paths`, `encode`, and `decode`) with pymoo, whose algorithms eliminate the proposals of designs proposed or evaluated before (`distinct`, by design rather than by point of the hypercube, `make_pymoo_problem`'s `duplicates`). Each design is evaluated once, and the designs proposed together are evaluated together, in parallel if the study is. A search returns its results and designs (`Search`), which `refine` and `confirm` refine and confirm as for any study. Constraints of a design space may return the amount of their violation (`Infeasible.violation`), which Optuna's trials record (`set_constraint`) for its constraint-aware samplers, and which is the constraint value of pymoo problems. Optional dependencies `tradespace` (joblib, Optuna, and pymoo).
 - Deployment of constellations (`tradespace.DeploymentSchedule`): from the rates and durations of a study (the mission and design life, the development of each type of satellite, its production on a line at a production rate, and launches at a cadence and capacity, in number or mass, optionally in an order of planes), each design's deployment (`DeploymentSchedule.plan`, a `Deployment` of its `Production`, `Launch`es, and replacements), so that a design of more satellites and planes takes longer to deploy. Its launches divide the mission into phases of constant sets of operating satellites (`Deployment.phases`), evaluated over the study's representative period from the observations of the full constellation (`evaluate_phases`) and combined over the mission (`summarize_phases`: the time-weighted mean of each metric and the time until it first meets a requirement).
 - Simulated missions with failures (`Deployment.simulate`, a `MissionHistory` for each replicate, drawn from its seed with common random numbers between designs): lives of satellites whose failure rates vary over the lifecycle (`tradespace.Exponential`, `Weibull`, `Bathtub` of competing causes, and `Piecewise` rates by age, `Lifetime`), failures of launches (`FailureModel`), and a policy that degrades or restores failed satellites (`RestorationPolicy`) by spares in orbit (taking a failed satellite's place after drifting), spares on the ground (launched at the next launch slot), or satellites produced when needed (after procurement, on the production line), optionally replenishing spares and batching restorations in launches. A history gives the phases in which satellites operate (`MissionHistory.phases`), measures of the mission (`MissionHistory.metrics`: failures, restorations, spares used, time to restore, and availability), and, priced by a `CostModel`, the cost of what it produces and launches. Policies are decisions of designs with a fragment of a design space (`policy_space`: whether to restore, spares in orbit and on the ground, and other fields of the policy, without spares that would never be used), and `configure` makes a policy (or a deployment schedule, or another dataclass) from the values of a design. Many histories of a replicate's mission (`Deployment.simulate_many`) share its sample of targets: `evaluate_missions` evaluates them (with their measures and costs) with a cache that evaluates each set of operating satellites once (`PhaseCache`, keyed by the content of the satellites and shared by the histories, policies, and designs of a replicate), and `summarize_missions` gives their means, quantiles (the risk of the mission), and probabilities of meeting requirements, as one record of the replicate. The histories of a replicate are stratified by default (a Latin hypercube over the histories of each satellite's life and each launch, drawn from uniform numbers by `Lifetime.from_uniform`), which estimates the means of missions 3 to 15 times more precisely than independent histories, and means may be estimated with control variates (`summarize_missions(controls=...)`, such as the availability of the mission, whose expected value simulations alone estimate, `Deployment.expected_metrics`). `allocate_histories` recommends the histories of each replicate (and the replicates, for a standard error) from a pilot with independent histories, by the variances between replicates and between histories and their costs (`summarize_missions(spread=True)` gives the standard deviations of the histories). `expected_missions` computes the expected capability and availability of a mission without restoration exactly (the sets of operating satellites by their probabilities, from the survival of each launch group, with planned replacements), without sampling error. Histories may be drawn from stressed failures for importance sampling (`simulate_many(stress=...)`: hazards and launch failures times a factor, each history weighted by its likelihood ratio censored at the end of the mission, `Lifetime.stressed` and `MissionHistory.weight`), which `summarize_missions` weighs in every statistic, with the effective number of histories: the probabilities of rare losses with 30 to 70 times less variance.
 - Life-cycle costs of deployments for trade studies (`tradespace.CostModel`): the development of each type of satellite, the production of its units on a learning curve (`SatelliteCost`), launches (per launch, or prices per satellite and kg, `LaunchCost`), operations (`OperationsCost`), replacements, and spares in orbit, dated by the deployment for their present value at a discount rate (`CostEstimate.present_value`, metric `cost present`) and annual costs (`CostEstimate.annual`, metric `cost peak annual`), with growth factors for cost risk (such as uncertain parameters, whose quantiles over replicates give the cost at a confidence level). TAT-C does not include cost-estimating relationships: the costs are given by the study. Fingerprints describe dataclasses (such as cost models) by their fields.
 - Function `tradespace.confirm` (and class `Confirmation`), which evaluates selected designs (such as a front) with new replicates that played no part in their selection, removing the optimism of the selection (the winner's curse, `Confirmation.optimism`). `ResultStore` keys evaluations by the fingerprint of a study (`Study.fingerprint`, a hash of `Study.definition`, see `get_definition`: its seed, use of common random numbers, design space, and build and evaluation functions, with the data frames they are given described by their content, the version of Python, and the source of the modules of TAT-C and resource files they depend on, found from their imports) and keeps each definition, so that variants of a study share a store without mixing their evaluations, and a study warns of evaluations of the same study with other code (of its functions, of TAT-C, or of Python), which are not reused, naming what differs (`fingerprint.compare_definitions`); `ResultStore.prune` removes them. Changes to TAT-C that a study does not depend on (such as to modules it does not use) keep its results. Namespaces of stores and caches are optional, for what fingerprints cannot see (data that functions read from files, and the code of other modules). `refine` treats designs with fewer than `min_replicates` (5) as uncertain whatever their probability, adds the replicates left to `max_replicates` in a last partial step, and warns if designs remain uncertain without a tolerance. `sensitivity` gives adjusted shares (epsilon squared) by default, without the share that the levels of a variable explain by chance (`adjusted=False` for correlation ratios).
 - The schedule and cost model of a `tradespace.MissionPlan` may be functions of the design (`schedule_of`, `cost_model_of`), which size them by its values with relationships of the study's own, such as the costs of a type of satellite from its payload or the capacity of launches to the design's altitude; launches limited in mass take the masses of the satellites from the cost model (`masses_of`, or the plan's `masses`). The `launch_mass_capacity` of a `DeploymentSchedule` may be a function of a plane (`mass_capacity_of`), for planes at different inclinations.
 - The start of the period of `tradespace.Observations` may be a value of the design (`start_of` and `end_of` give a design's period), such as an uncertain epoch drawn for each replicate over the mission, whose observations are cached apart, so that replicates average over the geometry and seasons of the mission that the one window of quasi-static phases misses (as `evaluate_phases` now states). The schedule of deployments states that a design life does not retire a satellite that is not replaced, and simulations that a planned replacement whose launch fails is not flown again.
 - Utilities of the metrics of designs (`tradespace.Utility`), for trade spaces of utility against cost: a single-attribute utility of each metric (`LinearUtility`, `ExponentialUtility` of a risk attitude, `PiecewiseUtility`, or any function), combined by weights additively or in Keeney and Raiffa's multiplicative form. A utility is a metric of any statistic (its `Mean` is the expected utility over the replicates, and a utility given as a statistic of `Criteria` is its mean), computed in each replicate, and gives the utility of each row of a data frame (such as a summary of the means of designs). Statistics name the columns they need (`Statistic.columns`).
 - Staged deployments (`tradespace.Stage`, argument `stages` of `DeploymentSchedule.plan` and `MissionPlan`, which may give the stages of each design): planes that are produced and launched only if a rule decides to, at times during the mission, from what it knows then (`Decision`: the satellites operating, the history so far, and, through the design, its uncertain parameters and scenario), after a lead time and at the next launch slots, and replaced and restored as other planes. Rules may also read the capability of the satellites operating at a decision (`Decision.capability`, the study's metrics of their observations, as of a phase), which the evaluations of an `ObservationStudy` give each deployment (`Deployment.capability`, a `tradespace.Capability` of its satellites, observations, and cached phases, which also caches the phases of studies without failures), such as for a plane deployed only if those in orbit fall short. A stage that is not deployed is neither produced, launched, nor priced; `MissionHistory.stages` gives when each was deployed, and its metrics the `stages deployed`. Exact expectations of missions without restoration (`expected_missions`) need deployments that are not staged, so that studies of staged deployments sample their histories.
 - Scenarios of studies (argument `scenarios` of `tradespace.Study`): conditions under which designs are compared rather than averaged over, each giving fixed values or uncertain parameters of the design space other values (`Design.realize` takes a `scenario`), so that each design is evaluated in each scenario (recorded as `scenario`, and judged in each with criteria `by` it). Each scenario's evaluations are stored by its values, so that a scenario added later is evaluated alone. Function `tradespace.robustness` gives each design's Pareto trace (the share of scenarios in which it is efficient) and its regret in each scenario (the improvement in every objective it would need to match the scenario's front) and the largest. The metrics of an `ObservationStudy` (and of `PhaseCache`, `evaluate_missions`, and `expected_missions`) are given the realized design if they have a parameter named `design`.
 - Criteria across the scenarios of a study (`tradespace.Across`, argument `across` of `Criteria`), which judge each design once by its worst or its (weighted) mean statistics over the scenarios (or the groups of another column), so that searches find designs robust to scenarios; a requirement may be met in every scenario while objectives are means.
 - Function `tradespace.attribute_variance` (and class `Attribution`), which attributes the variance of metrics between the replicates of each design to its uncertain parameters (or groups of them): the share that each causes, alone or with others, and the share that knowing it would remove, with standard errors, by evaluating designs with one parameter drawn as in other replicates and the others held (which works for seeds, whose values do not order outcomes); `Attribution.summary` pools the shares of several designs, and `Attribution.differences` attributes the variance of the differences between pairs of designs, from the same evaluations (parameters that act alike on both cancel, with common random numbers). `Design.realize` and `Study.run` take a `shift`, which draws named parameters as in other replicates (kept apart in a `ResultStore` as a variant of the replicates), and `DesignSpace.uncertain_paths` lists the uncertain parameters.
 - Structured cost risk (after Maier, Yeakel, and Wendoloski, "Cost Variance Analysis in Constellation Architecture Studies," 2023): growth factors of each type of satellite (by kind, for development, production, and launches, in `CostModel.estimate` and `MissionPlan.growth`), and the costs of types of satellites as sums of their components (`tradespace.Component`, `SatelliteCost.components`), whose growth has a distribution by maturity (such as `tradespace.Triangular`), correlated between components (`CostModel.correlation`). `CostModel.draw` draws the growth of the components from standard normal scores of the components and a common score, which `tradespace.risk_space` declares as uncertain parameters of a design space (named by `MissionPlan.scores`, also of a nested group), so that common random numbers give the designs that share a component the same growth of it; without drawn growth, components cost their expected growth. Types of satellites that share a component (by name) develop it once in a deployment. Production in blocks (`SatelliteCost.block_size`): each block after the first develops its type again (a share of its development, `redevelopment`) before its first unit is produced, and restarts the learning curve; `CostEstimate.blocks` counts the blocks of each type. Function `tradespace.exceedance` gives the probability that a metric of each design exceeds that of each other in the same replicate (such as that one design costs more than another), and `plot_exceedance` plots them as a matrix. The validation notebook `ValidateCostVarianceNSOSA` reproduces the paper's example of two NSOSA constellations that share a configuration.
 - Searches with Optuna accept categorical options that depend on earlier decisions (a parameter for each set of options), use TPE with a constant liar in batches by default (which spreads the trials of a batch), and stop after `max_designs` designs evaluated (trials that suggest designs evaluated before are told their objectives without evaluating them again). A design infeasible in some of its replicates violates its feasibility by their share (`Evaluations.infeasibility`), and the first design evaluated in a search is evaluated alone, to check that evaluations give one record each. `Study` records errors of evaluations as infeasible, without storing them, with `errors="record"`. Enumeration walks the items of unordered repetitions in one order only (the same designs, in the same order, four times faster for three sun-synchronous planes), and `DesignSpace.size` counts designs without keeping them. Pareto fronts are found by a vectorized sweep in lexicographic order that compares designs only with the efficient designs before them, and fronts with tolerances by comparing designs only with the efficient designs without tolerances (which dominate every design that any design dominates within the tolerances), in blocks of bounded memory: 20,000 designs in 0.05 s (0.11 s with tolerances) rather than 17 s, and the bootstraps of `pareto_probability` (and so `refine`) about 20 times faster.
 - Studies of the observations of constellations (`tradespace.ObservationStudy`): the targets of each replicate (`Targets`, fixed or generated from a seed of the design), the observations of each satellite, cached on disk (`Observations`), the metrics of the observations, and, with a `MissionPlan`, the costs of the deployment and the summaries of its phases or of the histories of its `Failures`. Plots of the results of studies (`plot_designs`, `plot_progress`, and `plot_means`, with matplotlib, now in the `tradespace` extra), and the hypervolume of a search as it proceeds (`hypervolume_progress`).
 - The steps of trade studies share their arguments and outcomes: `tradespace.refine(study, criteria, results)`, `confirm(study, criteria, results)` (by default, the front of the results), `screen(studies, criteria, designs)` (with criteria for each stage, in place of a `tolerance` of its own), and `attribute_variance(study, metrics, designs)` take designs as `Design` objects, their keys, or rows of a summary or of results, and `Refinement`, `Confirmation`, `Screening`, and `Search` give their `results`, the `summary` of their designs, and the `designs`. `DesignSpace.designs_of` rebuilds designs from rows of their values, which summaries keep (also those of `pareto_probability`, `confirm`, and `screen`), and `DesignSpace.extend`, `DesignSpace.design(design, **overrides)`, and `Study.replace` make variants of design spaces, designs, and studies. `aggregate` takes criteria, a mapping of objectives, or metrics (as the other functions do), and other `statistics` by name; `pareto_front`, `pareto_ranks`, `hypervolume`, `sensitivity`, and `main_effects` raise an error given the results of a study (a row per replicate) rather than a summary. Studies with a mission plan record the years by which it is `deployed`, and the parts of observation studies that refer to values of designs are typed as a `Reference`. Columns: `<metric> se` (rather than `sem`), `<metric> mission` for the mean over the phases of a mission, `<column> histories p<percent>` and `<column> histories std` for statistics over the histories of failures, and `pareto_trace` and `max_regret`. Package `tradespace` exports the functions and classes of trade studies; the parts from which they are made (such as `PhaseCache`, `Layout`, `configure`, and the fingerprints) are imported from its modules.
 - Example `SearchTradeSpace`, which searches a hierarchical trade space of constellations for the designs that sample points sufficiently: by evaluating every design, finding what drives the objectives, screening, and searching with Optuna and pymoo; and examples that take its trade further, importing its design space and study (`trade_space_example.py`): `RankUncertainDesigns` (the probability of efficiency, refinement, confirmation, and requirements on statistics), `CompareScenarios` (fronts, regret, and searches across scenarios), `TradeCostAndUtility` (cost at a confidence level, correlated cost risk of components, and utility), and `PlanMissions` (deployment, staging, failures, and the attribution of variance).
 - The `RunOSSEMonsoon` example declares its constellation trade as a design space, caches the looks of each satellite, evaluates its replicates in parallel, and finds its Pareto-efficient designs.

Changed:
 - Instruments reject unknown fields, rather than ignoring them: a misspelled field in the data of a `PointedInstrument` previously gave an `Instrument` with a field of regard of 180 degrees.
 - Dumping a `Satellite` or other system with a `PointedInstrument` or `ConicalInstrument` keeps the fields of its class, and loading the data restores it. Previously, these instruments were written and restored as a base `Instrument`, without a warning. Data written by earlier versions lacks these fields, so it still loads as an `Instrument`.
 - Dumping a space system with an `attitude` omits the `velocity_frame` of its instruments, which the attitude sets, so the data can be loaded.
 - TAT-C requires geopandas 1.0, pandas 2.0, shapely 2.0.1, and skyfield 1.49 or later, the first versions with features it uses; it did not work with the earlier minimums. The unit tests also run with these minimum versions, on Python 3.10.

Fixed:
 - `compute_projected_ray_position` of a rectangular view without along-track extent (a line across track) projected rays at angles of 180 and 360 degrees to the view's center, rather than its ends; likewise for a view without cross-track extent at 90 degrees. Rays of rectangular views at angles outside [0, 360) degrees, such as -90, were placed far from the view, and now match the equivalent angles. Instruments, whose fields of view must be positive, were not affected.
 - The footprint of a view of 180 degrees about nadir toward a target above the instruments (as from a `SurfacePlatform`) was, at some locations, the sky above the horizontal, which the view excludes, rather than the annulus between the horizontal and the horizon. Whether such a view contains nadir was judged from the mean direction of its edge, which lies on the edge; it is now judged from the center of the view.
 - `compute_latencies` no longer renames its columns in place, which pandas 3 deprecates with a warning. Tests and validation scripts no longer use `shapely.ops.transform`, which shapely 2.2 deprecates.
